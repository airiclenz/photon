## [Unreleased]

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-22

- Breaking: a storyboard's `camera` is now a mapping of two lists, `positions` (where the camera aims: a `cell` or a text target, with `at`, `hold` and `ease`) and `zooms` (`to`, `at`, `over` and `ease`), and `photon render` follows it: the camera holds each position, glides across the gap to the next one with that position's ease (a gap of 0 is a cut), and the crop is clamped inside the frame on every frame. A position's text target is looked up on the screen shown at the position's start only; when that screen does not show it, `photon render` fails under `camera.positions[i]`. The old `camera` list of `zoom` and `pan` moves, `via` paths and a zoom's `target` are gone and fail to load with the ordinary unknown-field error, and a beat's `zoom` is now refused as `beat <id>: zoom: removed; zoom with camera.zooms and aim with camera.positions`. `photon lint` reports a position's undeclared `area` under `camera.positions[i]`.
- Camera positions and zooms are now storyboard edit fields, so `photon tune` can change them: `position.at`, `position.hold`, `position.span` (at and hold together), `position.ease`, `position.cell` and `position.target` on an entry of `camera.positions`, and `zoom.at`, `zoom.over`, `zoom.ease` and `zoom.to` on an entry of `camera.zooms`. A hold of 0 or an ease of `smooth` removes the key, a cell replaces the position's `text`, `nth` and `area` and a target replaces its `cell`, and an edit that would make two positions or two zooms overlap is refused, naming both.
- Camera positions and zooms can now be added and removed by a storyboard edit, so `photon tune` can do it: `position.insert` adds a point in time on a cell (`{"at": <moment>, "cell": {"x", "y"}}`) and `zoom.insert` adds a zoom (`{"at": <moment>}`, written `to: 1.5`, `over: 600ms`) at the owner's index, creating `camera:` and the list when the storyboard has none; `position.remove` and `zoom.remove` remove the owner's entry, and removing a list's last entry deletes the list, and `camera:` with it when it holds no other list. An insert that overlaps another position or zoom is refused, naming both. Neither list can be reordered: entries play by their starts.
- A camera transition can now be moved by a storyboard edit, so `photon tune` can drag it: `position.transition`, owned by the position the transition leads into, takes `{"start_ms": s, "end_ms": e}` on the output clock and moves both edges at once. The position before it by start ends at `s` (a point moves its `at` there, a held position changes its `hold`), and the owner starts at `e` keeping its end (a point moves its `at`, a held position moves its `at` and changes its `hold`); each `at` keeps its beat. `s = e` is a hard cut. The edit is refused for the first position by start, for `s` after `e`, for an edge that would leave a negative hold, and for a result that overlaps another position.
- `photon tune`: the timeline has a **zoom** lane again, with one bar per `camera.zooms` entry labelled with the factor it zooms to. The lane's `+` button adds a zoom at the playhead. Dragging a bar moves that zoom: its `at` keeps its beat and only the offset changes. If the drag is refused (for example because it would overlap another zoom), the bar goes back where it was and the reason shows in the transport bar. Clicking a bar, or choosing the zoom in the inspector's select, opens a zoom panel where you can edit `at`, `over`, `to` and `ease` (smooth, linear, ease-in or ease-out) or remove the zoom. Errors on a zoom show on its own panel.
- `photon tune`: the timeline has a **position** lane. Each `camera.positions` entry draws as a bar over its hold, or as a point when it has no hold, and the gap between one position and the next by start draws as a **transition** bar in its own colour, so positions may be listed in any order. Clicking a position, or choosing it in the inspector's select, opens a position panel where you can edit `at`, `hold`, and its `cell` (x, y) or text target (`text`, `nth`, `area`), or remove it. Clicking a transition opens a transition panel showing where it starts and how long it lasts, both editable, and its ease (smooth, linear, ease-in or ease-out), which is the ease of the position it leads into. Errors on a position, and a refused transition edit, show on its panel. Position and transition bars cannot be dragged yet.
- `photon tune`: you can drag position and transition bars on the timeline's position lane, in 50ms steps. Dragging a position moves its `at`, keeping its beat. Dragging a transition moves both of its edges. Each bar has a grip on each edge. Dragging a position's end grip sets its `hold`. Dragging its start grip moves its `at` and changes its `hold` so its end stays put. A point has only an end grip, and dragging it right opens the point into a held position. Dragging a transition's start or end grip moves just that edge, and you can shrink a transition to 0, which makes it a hard cut. A transition has no grip on an edge that meets a point: that edge moves with the point's marker, so pressing the marker always selects or drags the point. Each drag sends one edit, which you can undo in one step. If the edit is refused, the bar goes back to where it was and the transport bar shows the reason.
- `photon tune`: the position lane has a `+` button that adds a camera position at the playhead. The new position is a point on the cell the camera is centred on at that moment, so adding it does not move the camera. If the add is refused, for example because the playhead is inside another position's hold, or the camera cannot be read, the transport bar shows the reason.
- `photon tune`: selecting a camera position moves the playhead to its start. While the position stays selected and the preview is paused, the preview shows the full view (the whole frame, whatever the camera's zoom and aim), with the camera's crop drawn as a dashed rectangle and a marker on the cell the position aims at. Drag the marker to any cell of the grid (it moves cell by cell and stops at the grid's edge); letting go on another cell sends one edit that sets the position's `cell`, replacing a text target, and the rectangle follows. Playing or scrubbing shows the normal preview frames without the overlay, and selecting anything else returns to the normal view. A pick made on the full view is resolved on the full view.
- `photon tune`: a pick's proposal now offers **Set as position target**, which sets the selected camera position's text target to the proposed one in one undoable edit, replacing its `cell` or earlier target. With no position selected the button is disabled, and its tooltip says to select a position. The Copy click, seen and drag buttons are unchanged.
- Breaking: the camera is now `camera.positions` and `camera.zooms` throughout photon, and the old top-level `camera:` list of `zoom` and `pan` moves never ships. `photon tune` edits the camera on a position lane, with a transition bar between each position and the next, and on a zoom lane; while a position is selected and the preview paused, the preview shows the full frame with the camera's crop and a marker you drag onto the cell the position aims at, and a pick's **Set as position target** sets the selected position's text target. The README (the `photon tune` section, the Commands table, Features, Captions, Targets and areas, and Sections and cursor), `photon tune --help` and `photon render --help` now describe camera positions and zooms. This replaces the earlier entries in this list about `pan` moves and the pan lane, `via` paths and the via-path overlay, the camera move panel and the camera-move problems shown on it, setting a pick as a pan's or a zoom's target, a camera zoom's `target`, and the `camera:` list of moves.
- `photon tune`: after an edit that makes the exact frame no wider than the shipped width, such as lowering `frame.scale`, the preview's draft frames come from the exact frame's rasters again instead of each snapshot being rasterized a second time.
- `photon tune`'s page no longer aims zooms: the pick's "Set as zoom target" button, the beat's zoom panel, the timeline's zoom lane and a camera zoom's target rows are gone; aim a camera zoom with a pan move.
- A camera `zoom` move no longer takes a `target`, and it holds only the zoom track. A storyboard whose camera zoom has a `target` is refused on load under that move, `camera[i]: zoom: target: removed; aim with a pan move over the same stretch`. To aim the camera, add a pan move over the same stretch.
- Breaking for storyboards written for v0.1.0: a beat no longer takes a `zoom`. A storyboard with a `zoom` on a beat is refused on load under that beat, `beat <id>: zoom: removed; use a camera zoom move and a pan move`. To zoom into a beat, add a camera `zoom` move over its stretch, and a `pan` move to aim it.
- The README, the glossary and `photon render --help` now describe zooming with camera `zoom` moves and aiming with `pan` moves only; the README's "Sections, zoom and cursor" section is now "Sections and cursor", its example storyboard zooms and pans with `camera` moves, and the Beats table no longer lists `zoom`.
- `photon tune` serves draft frames at `GET /api/frame?t=<ms>&draft=1`. A draft frame is drawn from a raster at the shipped size with a fast scaler, at the exact frame's size and with its cursor and captions, and is sent as an uncompressed PNG. Each session keeps the draft rasters in a 256 MiB cache. Without `draft`, or with `draft=0`, the route still answers the exact frame `photon render` composes. `photon tune --help` and the README say the preview shows draft frames while playing or scrubbing and settles on the exact frame once stopped.
- `photon tune`'s preview shows draft frames while it plays or while you scrub the timeline. When it stops (paused, stepped, jumped, scrub released or play ended), it shows a draft first and then replaces it with the exact frame `photon render` composes. If the exact frame is already on screen it is not requested again. An edit made while paused loads only the exact frame.
- `photon tune` fetches preview frames ahead of the playhead while it plays, up to 4 at a time, and shows a frame it already holds without asking the server again.
- `photon tune`: a refused add from a timeline lane's add button now shows its reason in the transport bar's status, whatever the inspector shows; the next accepted edit clears it.
- `photon tune`: a bar in the camera zoom, pan or captions lane can be dragged along the timeline to move that camera move or caption, keeping its length and snapping to 50ms. Letting go sends one edit: a camera move's `at` keeps its beat and its offset shifts, and a caption's `at` and `until` shift together. Clicking a bar without moving it still selects it. If a drag is refused, the bar goes back to where it was and the reason shows in the transport bar.
- `photon tune`: in a window too short for the preview, the transport bar and the timeline, the preview frame now shrinks (keeping its aspect ratio, never below 120px tall) so the timeline stays on screen without scrolling. Resizing the window re-fits it, a tall window shows it at full width again, and the pick box and via path stay aligned with the frame at every size.
- `frame.font_size`, `frame.line_height`, `frame.padding` and `frame.scale` are now storyboard edit fields, so `photon tune` can change them; `frame.font_size` must lie in the caption size range, 6..72, on load, lint and edit alike.
- `photon tune`'s Clip frame panel edits the frame's `font_size`, `line_height`, `padding` and `scale` beside `width` and `fps`, and re-renders the preview; `cols`, `rows` and `max_colors` still show read-only.
- `photon version` and `photon --version` now print the build string, such as `photon 0.1.0 (a1b2c3d)`: the version, the commit the binary was built from and `, dirty` when that tree had uncommitted changes. A binary built without VCS info, such as by `go run`, prints `photon 0.1.0`.
- `photon tune` shows the build serving the page, such as `photon 0.1.0 (a1b2c3d, dirty)`, the string `photon version` prints, muted at the right end of the transport bar, and `/api/state` carries it as `build`.
- A `caption_font.regular` or `caption_font.bold` that names a directory is now refused when the config loads, instead of failing later when the caption font is read.
- `photon tune` adds a zoom, a pan or a caption at the playhead from a `+` beside its lane, and the inspector moves the camera move or caption it shows up or down its list, or removes it.
- `photon tune`'s caption panel now edits a caption's style and placement: a font size, a regular or bold weight, colour pickers for the text and the box, a 0–1 slider for the box's opacity, fade in and fade out in milliseconds, and an x/y offset. Checking "explicit position" sets the caption's `position` to where its box sits now, with x/y inputs to move it; unchecking it drops the `position`, and the caption goes back to `place` and `align`. The preview frame reloads after each accepted edit.
- `photon tune`'s camera move panel edits a pan's path: a to/via select turns a straight pan into one along a `via` path and back, and a via pan lists each waypoint with cell x/y inputs and Set from pick, Insert after and Remove buttons. While a via pan is selected, the preview draws the path it runs on the frame shown.
- `golangci-lint` now enforces a 100-column wrap (a tab counts as 4) on non-test Go files through its `lll` linter; `_test.go` files are exempt.
- `photon render`, `photon tune` and `photon check` refuse a take whose beats started in another order than the storyboard's, naming both orders ("re-record it"); `render` and `tune` now word a take/storyboard beat mismatch as `check` does (`beat N: the take never started it`, `beat N: the take started it but the storyboard has no such beat`, `beat N: started twice in the take`) and report every mismatched beat, not just the first.
- `photon tune`: a camera move's or caption's error from rendering, such as a pan target that is not on screen or a caption box larger than the frame, now shows on that move's or caption's own panel and not on a beat or the clip. The Inspect list marks camera moves and captions that have an error standing.
- A storyboard whose every beat is cut is now refused when it loads (`beats: every beat is cut`), so `photon lint` reports it instead of a later step failing.
- `photon lint` now lays the captions out with the config's fonts and reports a caption whose box is larger than the frame, instead of leaving it for `photon render` to fail on.
- `photon tune`: a target's last match (`nth: last`) is kept when a target is set or copied.
- `photon tune`'s camera move panel sets a move's start with a beat (or clip time) select and an offset in milliseconds, a pan's target with text, nth and area inputs, and a pan's cell with x and y inputs, instead of YAML text.
- `photon tune`'s caption panel sets a caption's start and end with a beat (or clip time) select and an offset in milliseconds instead of YAML text; choosing "none" for the end turns an until into a `for` of the same length.
- `photon tune`: the inspector's Clip frame panel now shows the values of `cols`, `rows`, `padding`, `font_size`, `line_height`, `scale` and `max_colors`. They stay read-only.
- A storyboard may declare a top-level `camera:` list of zoom and pan moves placed on the clip's clock; `photon lint` reports every bad move, overlapping moves on one track and undeclared pan areas.
- Storyboards take a top-level `camera` list of zoom and pan moves placed on the clip's timeline (`at` a clip time or `{beat, offset}`, `over`, `ease`); a pan aims at a target or a `cell`, in a straight line or along a smooth `via` path.
- Source Sans 3 Regular and Bold (SIL Open Font License) are built in as the caption font, and the config's `caption_font: {regular, bold}` can name other font files instead; release archives also carry `SourceSans3-OFL.txt`.
- A storyboard may declare a top-level `captions:` list: text placed on the clip's clock (`at`, then `for` or `until`) with a font size and weight, a place and alignment or a position, colours, a background box and fades. `photon lint` reports every bad caption, such as a stop before its start, a time outside the clip, an unknown beat, a size out of bounds or fades longer than the caption.
- `photon render` and `photon tune` draw the storyboard's captions over every frame, on top of the cursor and in output space, so a zoom or pan never moves or scales them; they are drawn in the config's `caption_font` faces, or the built-in Source Sans 3.
- Storyboards can declare `captions`: text drawn over the shipped GIF on the clip's clock, with a font size and weight, a place or position, colours, a background box and fades. Captions use the built-in Source Sans 3 font, or the font files the config's `caption_font` names.
- `photon tune`'s timeline shows the storyboard's camera moves and captions as bars on `camera zoom`, `pan` and `captions` lanes under the beats; clicking a bar, or choosing it in the Inspect list, shows that entry's fields read-only in the inspector.
- `photon tune`'s inspector now edits a selected camera move's `at`, `over`, `ease` and `to`, and a selected caption's `text`, `at`, `for` or `until`, `place` and `align`; a pick can be set as the selected pan's `to`.
- `photon tune`: a target picker on the preview. Press ⌖ Pick and drag across the cells of one row on the paused frame. The page proposes a target that the resolver has checked and draws its box. You can copy it as click, seen, drag-from or drag-to YAML.
- `photon tune <storyboard> [<take>]` serves a timing editor on `127.0.0.1` (`--port`, default any free port) and prints its address. The page previews the take: quick draft frames while it plays or scrubs, and the exact frame `photon render` composes once it stops. You can edit each beat's `duration`, `hold` and `cut` and the clip's `frame.width` and `frame.fps`, with undo, redo and revert. Save writes only the edited values back into the storyboard, so comments and layout stay. Ctrl-C stops it.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-17

- `photon record <storyboard>` runs your terminal app in a pty, performs the storyboard's beats (humanized typing, named keys, real mouse clicks and drags, waits) and saves every screen change as a take, so a re-record after the UI changes is one command instead of a hand performance.
- `photon check <storyboard> [<take>]` judges a take against the storyboard's expects and prints a check table; `record` checks every take it writes and exits 0 only when every beat and expect passed, so `until photon record ...; do :; done` is the whole retake loop.
- `photon render <storyboard> [<take>]` turns a take into the shipped GIF: each beat becomes a section of exactly its `duration`, played at 1×, held or sped up to fit, and a `cut` beat is dropped from the clip. `--dry-run` prints the ffmpeg command lines instead, and memory stays flat however long the clip.
- Services and profiles: `photon.yaml` can start helper processes, such as a fake backend, before the launch, wait until each is ready, hand out their ports as `PHOTON_PORT_<NAME>` and stop them after the take; `photon record --profile <name>` swaps their commands or env and runs an `on_success` command after a good take.
- Targets and areas: clicks, drags and zooms aim at on-screen text found by a regex, optionally within a named band of rows that is found afresh on every screen, so a storyboard follows the app's layout as it moves.
- A zoom pushes into its target and eases back out within its section, and a drawn cursor glides to each click, rings on the press and follows the button through a drag.
- Frames are drawn with fonts embedded in the binary (Source Code Pro with Noto Sans Symbols 2, Math and Symbols as fallbacks, under the SIL Open Font License), so a clip renders the same on every machine; a project can supply its own faces through the config's `fonts` directory.
- `photon lint <storyboard>` prints every problem in a storyboard and its config without recording anything, and `photon setup <storyboard>` runs the config's `setup` once to build the rig.
- `photon version` and `photon --version` print photon's version.
- Maintainers can cut a release with `go run ./cmd/release` (`dist`, `status`, `prepare`, `publish`, `smoke`), and bumping `VERSION` on `main` now tags the release automatically.
- Every release archive carries the SIL Open Font License texts of the embedded fonts (`SourceCodePro-OFL.txt`, `NotoSansMath-OFL.txt`, `NotoSansSymbols-OFL.txt`) beside `LICENSE` and `README.md`.
- Installing photon with Homebrew or Scoop now installs ffmpeg with it, so `photon render` works out of the box; gifsicle stays optional.
- photon installs with Homebrew (`brew install airiclenz/tap/photon`), with Scoop from the `airiclenz/scoop-bucket` bucket, or from the release archives on GitHub, beside `go install`; `docs/releasing.md` describes how a release is cut.
